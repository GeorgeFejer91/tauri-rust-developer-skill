## Status Vocabulary

- **adopted**: full or sufficiently complete source inspected; durable guidance integrated.
- **confirmed**: source independently supports guidance already present; no duplicate prose added.
- **partial**: only part of the source or metadata/transcript was accessible; use only for claims actually inspected.
- **rejected**: stale, unsafe, misleading, overly thin, or irrelevant.
- **queued**: discovered but not yet read deeply enough to use.

## Search and Access Limitations

- Twenty-one English YouTube subtitle tracks plus matching metadata were downloaded and read. A later targeted sweep found additional long-form Android, sidecar, plugin, testing, and conference videos, but YouTube began returning HTTP 429 for caption endpoints; titles alone were not treated as evidence.
- “All tutorials online” is not a finite enumerable set. Search used broad and topic-specific queries across official docs, GitHub, written tutorials, community books, and YouTube until repeated queries produced duplicates, marketing comparisons, or patterns already covered. This ledger makes that saturation boundary auditable.
- Search snippets were discovery aids only. A source received `adopted` status only after its accessible page, transcript, PDF text/render, documentation, or repository content was inspected.

## Research Notes

- Current Tauri end-to-end tooling and platform coverage change quickly; reject tutorials that encode historical WebDriver limitations without checking current official docs.
- Prefer extracting video subtitles/transcripts and inspecting code repositories over relying on titles or descriptions.
- Treat claimed application size, speed, or Electron comparisons as marketing unless reproducible measurements and build conditions are supplied.
- A recent date and polished code are not evidence that an API exists; require official documentation, a generated schema, crate/package source, or a compiling minimal reproduction.
